04 / Worked examples

Examples

See the same counting idea appear in everyday situations, pure mathematics, and computer science.

Example 1 / Basic principle

Gloves in a drawer

A drawer contains black and white gloves. How many gloves must you take to guarantee two of the same color?

Pigeons: the gloves you take.

Holes: the 2 colors, black and white.

With 3 gloves and 2 colors, the calculation is ceil(3 / 2) = 2.

Therefore, 3 gloves guarantee two gloves of the same color.

Example 2 / Generalized principle

Books on shelves

If 23 books are placed on 6 shelves, what can you guarantee about one shelf?

Pigeons: 23 books.

Holes: 6 shelves.

The generalized principle gives ceil(23 / 6) = 4.

Therefore, at least one shelf contains 4 or more books.

Example 3 / Real life

Birthdays in a group

A club has 25 members. What must be true about their birthdays?

Pigeons: 25 members.

Holes: 12 months of the year.

The calculation is ceil(25 / 12) = 3.

Therefore, at least 3 members share a birth month.

Example 4 / Number theory

Remainders

Among 8 integers, what can you guarantee about their remainders when divided by 7?

Pigeons: the 8 integers.

Holes: the 7 possible remainders, 0 through 6.

Since 8 > 7, two integers must have the same remainder.

Therefore, their difference is divisible by 7.

Example 5 / Computer science

Hash collisions

A hash table has 100 buckets. What is unavoidable when 101 different keys are inserted?

Pigeons: the 101 keys.

Holes: the 100 hash buckets.

There are more keys than buckets: 101 > 100.

Therefore, at least two keys must hash to the same bucket: a collision.
